# 2027 Med charter lists hold flat, discounts deepen

For 2023-or-newer hulls, the 23-model Nautical Nomads fleet spans a bareboat peak week of roughly **€5,800 (Sun Odyssey 440) to €20,900 (Lagoon 51)**, with the two crewed-only boats (Lagoon 55, Bali 5.4) at **€29,000–35,000 per week including crew but before APA**; low-season (April / late-October) weeks sit at **0.35–0.55 of peak** for most models. Every 2027 price list already published is **flat to +6% on 2026** (Certain for the five same-boat pairs observed; Likely fleet-wide), so 2026 tables can be rolled forward where 2027 is unpublished. Four models — Saona 47, Bavaria Cruiser 46, Oceanis 48 and Sun Odyssey 519 — have **no 2023+ hulls in charter** and are priced on their successors (Tanna 47, Bavaria C46, Oceanis 46.1, Sun Odyssey 490); the Lagoon 55 and new Oceanis Yacht 60 exist only as crewed or skippered product. On top of list, the agency must apply the operator's season calendar (Greece 8–9 coarse steps, Croatia near-weekly, Italy/Spain every week, Turkey peaking in September), early-booking discounts of **10–20% to 31 Dec / 31 Jan capped at 15–20% total**, mandatory extras of **€300–800 per charter plus €2,000–5,000 deposits**, skipper fees of **€1,400–2,000 per week**, and the convention that Booking Manager/NauSYS prices are **gross end-client prices inclusive of VAT and commission**, from which the agency retains **15–20% on bareboat and 10–15% on crewed**.

Four substitutions need explaining to clients and to the spreadsheet's users. The **Saona 47** ended production around 2021: the Tanna 47 was launched as a redesign of the Saona with the same rig, displacement and deck layout ([Multihulls World](https://www.multihulls-world.com/news-catamaran-trimaran/tanna-47-the-saona-reviewed-and-updated)), so the Tanna figures are a like-for-like 2023+ price; the only 2020 Saonas still chartering list €13,000 peak in Trogir and €12,320 in Lavrion, 10% under the 2025 Tanna in the same Trogir marina ([yacht-rent Saona 47 Trogir](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/66744-fountaine-pajot-saona-47-trogir)). The **Oceanis 48** exists in charter only as 2013–2018 builds — yacht-rent's model page lists 50 boats, none newer than 2018 ([yacht-rent Oceanis 48 model page](https://www.yacht-rent.com/boat-models-in-charter/oceanis-48)) — and the 2023+ Oceanis 46.1 used instead carries a 15–30% higher peak than a 2018 Oceanis 48 (a 2024 5-cabin 46.1 in Split publishes €6,300 for 24 Jul–7 Aug 2027) ([yacht-rent Oceanis 46.1 Split](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/66787-oceanis-46.1-split)). The **Bavaria Cruiser 46** has no 2023+ hulls in any listing found; the C46 used instead is the current-production equivalent, and its 5-cabin version prices 15–25% above the 4-cabin.

Three further caveats on the headline numbers. The **Lagoon 43 peak (€14,818)** is pulled up by a 2025 boat in Palma publishing €18,335 for 31 Jul–14 Aug 2027 ([yacht-rent Lagoon 43 Mallorca](https://www.yacht-rent.com/spain/74514-lagoon-43-mallorca)); Croatian and Greek 2026-build Lagoon 43s list €11,400–11,600 at peak, essentially level with the Lagoon 46. The **Elan Impression 50.1 low of €1,592** comes from yield-managed Trogir boats and a budget Pirovac operator priced at half the Trogir level; treat anything below ~€2,800 as a distressed week, not a list price. The **Jeanneau 60 figures (€3,235 / €9,581)** rest on two 12knots listings whose 2027 rows very probably embed a ~20–22% early-booking discount (demonstrated for the Hanse 590 below), so the undiscounted list peak is closer to €12,000 — consistent with a 2021 five-cabin Jeanneau 60 in Athens listing €14,250 ([yacht-rent Jeanneau 60 Athens](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/31085-jeanneau-60-athens)).

## Greece steps eight times, Croatia weekly, Italy and Spain every week

No operator in the sample publishes named "Low A / High / Peak" bands; each publishes a stepped list and the step dates are the bands (Certain). The shape differs sharply by country, and an agency quoting across five markets must hold five calendars. **Greece** uses a coarse, stable 8–9-step calendar: the Lefkas Oceanis 46.1 2027 list steps at 17 Apr, 15 May, 12 Jun, 24 Jul, 14 Aug, 11 Sep, 25 Sep and 2 Oct, running €3,780 → €4,100 → €5,230 → €5,950 → €6,700 (peak 24 Jul–14 Aug) and back down symmetrically, i.e. ratios of **0.56 / 0.61 / 0.78 / 0.89 / 1.00**, with every price unchanged from 2026 ([yacht-rent Oceanis 46.1 Lefkas](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/62823-oceanis-46.1-lefkas)). **Croatia** steps almost every Saturday from mid-May to late September: the 2027 Trogir Lagoon 47 list runs from a 0.42 floor (3 Apr–8 May) through 0.55–0.58 in late May, 0.75–0.84 in June, a five-week peak 3 Jul–7 Aug, then 0.83 → 0.72 → 0.60 through August and September before dropping back to 0.42 on 25 Sep ([12knots Lagoon 47 Trogir](https://12knots.com/de/yacht-charter/lagoon-47-active-cruises-67625/)). **Italy and the Balearics** price every single week and have the steepest curves: a Sardinian Dufour 470 runs 0.38–0.43 of peak in April, 0.62–0.76 in June, with the absolute top on 15–22 August ([yacht-rent Dufour 470 Sardinia](https://www.yacht-rent.com/italy/53292-dufour-470-sardinia)), and a Palma Dufour 460 sits at only 0.28–0.32 in April and peaks 8–15 August ([yacht-rent Dufour 460 Mallorca](https://www.yacht-rent.com/spain/26603-dufour-460-gl-mallorca)). **Turkey** inverts the pattern: the one 2027 Fethiye list found is flat at 0.82 from 24 April to 4 September and peaks (1.00) from 4 September to 23 October ([12knots Bavaria 46 Fethiye](https://12knots.com/en/yacht-charter/bavaria-cruiser-46-4-cab-sail-betelgeuse-34358/)), matching 12knots' Turkey-wide averages where September is the dearest month and April the cheapest. ## Early booking strips 10–20% off list, capped at 15–20%, and aggregators already net it out

The early-booking convention is uniform in structure and varies only in size and deadline (Certain for the sample, Likely fleet-wide). The agency platform yacht-rent states early-booking savings of up to 20% for Mediterranean bookings confirmed by the last day of January, with higher "first minute" reductions for bookings made by end-December ([yacht-rent early booking](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/early-booking)). Operator-level terms cluster in three families. The Marina Baotić/Trogir and Palma fleet offers **15% until 15 December, 12% until 15 January**, plus 5%/10% for two/three weeks, 5% repeat and 5% multi-boat, all capped at 15% ([yacht-rent Lagoon 42 Trogir](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/71484-lagoon-42-trogir)). Most Croatian, Italian and some Greek operators offer **10% until 31 December** stepping to 5% until 28 February with a 15–20% cap ([yacht-rent Lagoon 51 Split](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/61501-lagoon-51-split-region)). Several Greek fleets offer **15% until 31 March** with a 15% cap ([yacht-rent Lagoon 46 Lefkas](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/51375-lagoon-46-lefkas)). The most aggressive Croatian monohull operators go to **20% until 31 December**, and one Split Elan 45 offers 20% to 31 December then 15% to 28 February with a 25% combined cap ([yacht-rent Elan Impression 45 Split](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/72660-elan-impression-45-split)). The Moorings benchmark is 10% for bookings by 2 November 2026 on 2027 starts, peak dates excluded, plus 5% loyalty ([Moorings early-booking offer](https://www.moorings.com/de/angebote/fruehbucherangebot)). Discounts apply to the base charter fee only, never to transit log, deposit or crew, which are listed as fixed amounts on every list sampled (Likely).

The trap for a new agency is that **aggregator prices are frequently already net**. The same Hanse 458 lists €2,300 off-season on yacht-rent and $1,620 on 12knots, which at the site's own implied rate is €1,450 — exactly the €2,300 list less the 37% shown — while its peak week is only ~27% below list ([12knots Hanse 458 Endless Seas](https://12knots.com/en/yacht-charter/hanse-458-endless-seas-43146/)). The same Hanse 590 lists a $16,669 peak on Samboat (gross, with a separately stated 20% owner promotion) and $12,935 on 12knots with a "-22%" tag ([Samboat Hanse 590 Volare](https://www.samboat.co.uk/boat-hire/kastela/sailboat/182949); [12knots Hanse 590 Volare](https://12knots.com/en/yacht-charter/hanse-590-volare-41969/)). The embedded discount is not uniform across the season — larger off-peak, smaller at peak — so dividing a 12knots row by (1 − headline %) over-states peak list. The spreadsheet's figures follow the notes' convention of using yacht-rent/Samboat/Pitter EUR lists as gross and treating 12knots rows as net-of-early-booking; the Jeanneau 60 and Oceanis Yacht 60 are the models where this matters most.

Last-minute practice is a per-week markdown rather than a rule: the 2026 Trogir Saona 47 showed 35% off 13–20 June, 20% off 27 June–4 July and 15% off most July, August and October weeks ([yacht-rent Saona 47 Trogir](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/66744-fountaine-pajot-saona-47-trogir)), Sardinian lists 41–58% off nearly every unsold week, and 1a-yachtcharter states cuts "of up to 50%" are already inside its displayed 2027 prices ([1a-yachtcharter Bavaria C46](https://en.1a-yachtcharter.de/charter/bavaria-c46)). Model 15–40% on Croatian/Greek weeks published 4–12 weeks out (Likely).

## Extras add €300–800 per charter; skippers €1,400–2,000 a week; crewed boats add 25–40% APA

Mandatory extras are country-specific and must be quoted as fixed add-ons outside the discounted fee (Certain). In **Croatia**, the old "transit log" has largely become a bundled pack: the Baotić/Trogir fleet charges €20 handling + €355 preparation fee (welcome pack, final cleaning, linen, dinghy and outboard, first and last marina night) + €1 per person per day sojourn tax, with a €4,000 deposit ([yacht-rent Lagoon 42 Trogir](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/71484-lagoon-42-trogir)); monohulls in the 44–51 ft band run €250–500, and a Lagoon 51 carries a €700/week comfort pack plus, where the skipper is mandatory, €1,750/week skipper and €1,000/week APA covering fuel and the captain's meals ([yacht-rent Lagoon 51 Split](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/61501-lagoon-51-split-region)). Guides put the Croatian transit log at €250–400 by length, tourist tax at about €1 per person per night and deposits at €1,500–3,500 refundable or €150–300 non-refundable insurance ([Yachtic Croatia 2026 guide](https://blog.yachtic.com/en/where-to-go/en-yacht-charter-croatia-2026-guide-2/)). In **Greece**, the Lefkas convention is a €300 start pack (cleaning, gas, water, linen, first and last night harbour fees) paid in cash on arrival, a €3,000 deposit, or €300 non-refundable insurance plus €600 refundable — the insurance being mandatory on skippered charters ([yacht-rent Oceanis 46.1 Lefkas](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/62823-oceanis-46.1-lefkas)); Istion-fleet boats instead impose a ~€400 damage waiver plus ~€230 charter pack. **Italy** publishes lump-sum "obligatory services" of €600–725 with €3,500 deposits; **Palma** bundles more into the fee (a Lagoon 43 lists €25 handling + €380 preparation, €4,000 deposit, and a compulsory €55/day damage waiver whenever a skipper is added) ([yacht-rent Lagoon 43 Mallorca](https://www.yacht-rent.com/spain/74514-lagoon-43-mallorca)); **Turkey** charges a ~$445 service pack with a ~$3,000 deposit ([12knots Bavaria 46 Fethiye](https://12knots.com/en/yacht-charter/bavaria-cruiser-46-4-cab-sail-betelgeuse-34358/)). Catamaran packs run higher than monohull packs in the same marina (€430–480 for a Lagoon 42/43/46 in Croatia versus €250–390 for a 45–50 ft monohull), and deposits scale with size: €2,000–3,000 for 44–46 ft monohulls, €3,000–5,000 for 51–60 ft and for cats (Likely).

Skipper and hostess rates are published per day or per week; the guest provides food and a cabin. Greek operators charge **€210/day skipper and €190/day hostess**, cooks €190–230 ([yacht-rent Oceanis 46.1 Athens](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/46079-oceanis-46.1-athens)); Croatian lists show ≈€255/day skipper and ≈€223/day hostess on the Trogir Lagoon 47 ([12knots Lagoon 47 Trogir](https://12knots.com/de/yacht-charter/lagoon-47-active-cruises-67625/)); Turkish lists $223 and $134. The new 60-footers are effectively skippered-only: the 2025 Oceanis Yacht 60 at Baotić mandates a $257/day skipper and a $637 transit-log pack on top of its €6,900–16,600 boat price ([Filovent Oceanis 60 Scorpio III](https://www.filovent.com/us/sailboat/oceanis-60/141557.html)). For planning, add **€1,500–2,000 per week for a skipper** and **€1,200–1,550 for a hostess**, plus roughly €25–35/day food each (Guessing — no list publishes the allowance).

Crewed pricing follows a different grammar. The crewed Lagoon 55 "Artisan" in Split publishes 2027 boat prices of ≈€14,200 (spring) to ≈€33,000 (26 Jun–21 Aug) with half-board included, then adds a mandatory **APA of $8,938 per week**, a $950 comfort package and tourist tax ([12knots Lagoon 55 Artisan](https://12knots.com/en/yacht-charter/lagoon-55-luxury-version-2025-artisan-16427/)); the crewed Bali 5.4 in Mallorca adds a fixed **€7,000 APA** to a €23,540–31,350 fee ([yacht-rent Bali 5.4 Mallorca](https://www.yacht-rent.com/spain/51127-bali-5.4-mallorca)), while one Greek Bali 5.4 bundles APA and VAT into the boat price. Brokerage convention is APA of 25–35% of the fee for sailing yachts and cats ([HELM, What is an APA](https://www.helm.yt/article/what-is-an-apa)); Croatian crewed brokers quote **ex-13% VAT with APA "usually about 40%"** ([Yachts Croatia Jeanneau Yachts 60](https://www.yachtscroatia.com/luxury-sailing-yacht-charter-croatia/jeanneau-yachts-60)), and gratuity of 10–20% is customary. Every crewed quote therefore needs three questions answered — VAT in or out, APA fixed or percentage, catering included or not — before it can be compared with a bareboat-plus-skipper build-up.

## Commission is 15–20% of a gross, VAT-inclusive price — and 2027 lists are flat on 2026

The commercial convention that the agency builds its margin on is documented by Booking Manager, which also serves NauSYS-style agency portals. Each operator sets its own commission; **usual Mediterranean rates are about 15–20% for bareboat and 10–15% for crewed**, shown per offer in the search, and agencies collect payment from the client and transfer it to the operator after an option-then-booking flow ([Booking Manager Agency Portal FAQ](https://support.booking-manager.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013721880-Frequently-Asked-Questions-about-the-Agency-Portal)). Critically, "all prices must include taxes (VAT) and agency commission" and "prices displayed in the system always need to be end client prices" ([Booking Manager Charter Operators FAQ](https://support.booking-manager.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013794919-Frequently-Asked-Questions-for-Charter-Operators)) — so published price = gross, the client pays gross less any discount, and the agency remits gross-after-discount × (1 − commission). Two inferences follow (Likely, not documented): commission is earned on the discounted charter fee only, not on transit logs, deposits or crew, which every list shows as fixed separately-paid items; and any discount beyond the operator's stated cap comes out of the agency's commission. Payment terms are consistently 50% on booking and 50% at 30 days (Greece, Italy), four weeks (Croatia) or 60 days (Spain) before departure ([yacht-rent Oceanis 46.1 Athens](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/46079-oceanis-46.1-athens)). On a €10,000 peak-week Lagoon 46 with 10% early booking and 17% commission, the arithmetic is: client pays €9,000 plus ~€430 pack plus tax and deposit; agency retains €1,530; operator receives €7,470.

The 2027 trend evidence supports rolling 2026 lists forward with little or no uplift. Where the same boat shows both years, the Lefkas Oceanis 46.1 is unchanged in every period ([yacht-rent Oceanis 46.1 Lefkas](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/62823-oceanis-46.1-lefkas)); a Mallorca Bavaria C46 publishes identical 2026 and 2027 tables ([yacht-rent Bavaria C46 Mallorca](https://www.yacht-rent.com/spain/68534-bavaria-c46-balearic-islands)); the Split Lagoon 51 "Reality" is **+5.8% at peak** (€21,800 vs €20,600) with a longer seven-week peak period ([yacht-rent Lagoon 51 Split](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/61501-lagoon-51-split-region)); the Split Oceanis 46.1 is ~6% above its yield-managed 2026 peak; and a Greek Bavaria C57 raised low and mid-season weeks 10–21% while holding peak flat ([yacht-rent Bavaria C57 Athens](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/75150-bavaria-c57-athens)). Against that, a 2024 Lagoon 46 in Rogoznica cut its peak 2% from 2025 to 2026 as the boat aged a year ([yacht-rent Lagoon 46 Rogoznica](https://www.yacht-rent.com/croatia-charter/58747-lagoon-46-rogoznica)), and a new 2026 Dufour 54 lists only 3–5% above the 2023 Dufour 530 at the same Tuscan base ([yacht-rent Dufour 54 Grosseto](https://www.yacht-rent.com/italy/71340-dufour-54-grosseto)). Industry context is recalibration rather than growth: average Mediterranean charter spend is stabilising near €5,500, down from €6,000+, with higher prices and marina fees capping demand in lower-budget catamaran segments ([Booking Manager, State of the Industry 2025](https://www.booking-manager.com/blog/state-of-the-yacht-charter-industry-2025.html)). Build-year matters less than operator positioning for boats under four years old — a 2023 Lagoon 40 in Athens lists ~19% above a 2018 sister at peak, yet a 2021 Lagoon 42 in Lefkas out-prices a 2025 one at the same port in low season ([yacht-rent Lagoon 40 Athens](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/54059-lagoon-40-athens); [yacht-rent Lagoon 42 Lefkas](https://www.yacht-rent.com/greece/74522-lagoon-42-lefkas)) — so a 0–5% per-year age discount for 2023–2026 hulls is Guessing from two pairs.

## Conclusion

The fleet divides into three commercial products that should not share one pricing template: eighteen genuine bareboats where list, early-booking cap and 15–20% commission are mechanical; three large monohulls (Jeanneau 60, Oceanis Yacht 60, Hanse 588/590) where the published 2027 numbers are thin, mostly skippered, and in the Jeanneau's case almost certainly already discounted, so quotes should be built from Samboat/Pitter-style gross lists rather than 12knots; and two crewed cats (Lagoon 55, Bali 5.4) where the headline is only half the client's bill once APA, VAT treatment and catering are resolved. For the four models with no 2023+ hulls, the honest sales line is that the client is being quoted a current-production successor — and for the Sun Odyssey 519 that successor is a smaller boat.

Two implications for an agency opening in the 2027 selling window. First, most Croatian and Greek 2027 lists will reach the booking systems in November–December 2026 at flat to +6% on 2026, so a spreadsheet built on 2026 EUR lists is already within early-booking noise of the truth; the leverage lies in the discount calendar (15 Dec, 31 Dec, 15 Jan, 31 Mar), not in forecasting list prices. Second, because the season shapes diverge — Greece flat and September-strong, Croatia weekly-stepped with a five-to-seven-week peak, Italy and Spain priced week-by-week to a mid-August spike, Turkey peaking in autumn — the cheapest honest differentiator is a shoulder-season product (Greek Ionian in September, Croatian mid-June, Turkish May) where list is 0.6–0.8 of peak and last-minute markdowns of 15–40% are routine.
